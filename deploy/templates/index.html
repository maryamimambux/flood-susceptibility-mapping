About the Project

Predicting Flood Risk from Space

The 2022 Sindh floods displaced over 7 million people and submerged roughly one-third of Pakistan. This project builds a flood susceptibility model that learns environmental patterns from the 2022 event to predict which areas are most prone to flooding.

An XGBoost classifier was trained on 500,000 pixel samples across the Sindh province, using ground truth from Copernicus Emergency Management Service (EMS) Rapid Mapping delineation products. The model evaluates seven geospatial predictors at 30-meter resolution.

Elevation
Copernicus DEM 30m, reprojected to UTM Zone 42N
Slope
Terrain gradient derived from pit-filled DEM via WhiteboxTools
TWI
Topographic Wetness Index from D8 flow accumulation
Dist. to River
Euclidean distance to HydroRIVERS v10 network
Rainfall Max
Peak pentad rainfall from CHIRPS-2.0, August 2022
Rainfall Cum.
Cumulative monthly rainfall across six pentads
Land Cover
Placeholder layer (ESA WorldCover pending)

Data Sources
Copernicus DEM 30m (AWS S3) · CHIRPS-2.0 Rainfall · HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS v10 · Copernicus EMS Rapid Mapping (EMSR629, EMSR631)

Limitations
This is a susceptibility model, not a real-time forecast. Temporal dynamics such as flood wave propagation and dam releases are not captured. Trained on a single event (August 2022).
